# Gamma Regime: Negative Gamma

## Background: Dealer Positioning

- Options dealers constantly hedge their exposure. When traders buy options, dealers typically take the opposite side of those positions.
- To stay neutral, dealers adjust their hedge as price moves. These adjustments create buying and selling pressure in the underlying market.
- Heatseeker shows this exposure as nodes across strikes. The nodes show where dealer positioning is concentrated.
- Understanding dealer positioning lets traders anticipate how dealers are likely to hedge when price moves.

## Definition

A negative gamma regime happens when dealers are short gamma. It is a fast, volatile environment.

| Attribute | Negative Gamma |
|---|---|
| Dealer position | Short gamma |
| Heatmap color | Purple (barney) nodes |
| Dealer hedging | Dealers hedge with the move. Rising prices force dealers to buy. Falling prices force dealers to sell. |
| Effect on volatility | Amplifies price movement. Purple nodes increase volatility and speed up price action. |
| Market type | Expanding, momentum-driven |
| Feel | Fast |
| Think of it like | Price is free to move violently through the map |

## What You'll Notice

- Price moves quickly
- Node overshoots happen more often
- Levels can bounce or reject fast
- Air pockets get filled aggressively
- Fast, aggressive candles with strong momentum
- "Wicky" price action
- Air pockets between nodes

## Behavior at Map Levels

Applies to the King Node, floors, ceilings, and gatekeepers.

- Floors can knife through
- Ceilings can get punched through
- Price moves fast between zones
- When price rejects a ceiling, it tends to dump quickly

You'll often see:
- Sharp pushes
- Fast travel through weak areas

## Typical Day Types

### Trend Day (Usually Negative Gamma)

**What it looks like:**
- Charts sitting on a key level
- Maps with nodes far from spot price, with rapid accumulation
- Air pockets present
- A massive King Node that grows rapidly over time
- Floors and ceilings roll up or down
- Velocity mode shows clear, one-directional positioning and rapid node accumulation in one direction

**Velocity mode:** Shows the % change of nodes. It gives real-time insight into where dealers are actively repositioning.

**How to think:**
- Don't fade strength blindly
- If you missed the entry, sit out until a clear pivot appears (for example, a King Node price target getting hit)
- Focus on structure in the direction of the move

**Rolling of ceilings and floors:**

| Event | What happens | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling ceiling | The upside ceiling and/or upside price targets decrease in value, and the ceiling moves to a lower strike | Strong presumptive evidence of a bearish thesis playing out |
| Rolling floor | The downside floor and/or downside price targets decrease in value, and the floor moves to a higher strike | Strong presumptive evidence of a bullish thesis playing out |

Understanding these market maker behaviors shows when a trend is starting to form or when momentum is building in one direction.

### Whipsaw Day (Negative Gamma + Air Pockets)

**What it looks like:**
- Fast moves through predominantly negative gamma nodes
- Disorganized, chaotic maps
- No confluence among the trinity maps
- Violent reversals off the extreme ends of ranges
- "Wicky" price action

**How to think:**
- Play the extreme ends of ranges
- Wait for clarity to form
- When in doubt, sit out

## Common Mistakes

1. **Fading everything in negative gamma:** Trying to catch reversals in fast conditions. This leads to drawdown during overshoots and force-closing at a loss right before the trade reverses in your favor.
2. **Ignoring regime:** Trading every day the same way.
3. **Skipping charts:** Jumping straight to Heatseeker.

## General Rules (Apply to Both Regimes)

### Regime Does Not Give Direction

- Regime does not tell you where price goes. Node accumulation and map reshuffles tell you where price is likely to go next.
- Regime tells you how price is likely to move between levels.
- Identifying the regime gives context for what to expect during the day, which helps you judge entries and exits.
- You can be right on direction, but without proper execution it will not matter.

### The Absolute Value Rule

- Do not focus only on color. The largest absolute value nodes can be either purple or yellow.
- The larger the absolute value, the more intense the color. Larger values hold more strength.
- Nodes act like magnets. The bigger the node, the stronger the pull. The closer the node, the stronger the pull.
- Large nodes are where dealers have the most exposure, so they are more likely to influence price.
- A very large purple node may have more influence than a small yellow node, and the reverse can also be true. A large yellow node can create strong support or resistance regardless of the nodes around it.
- **Rule:** The magnitude of a node determines its influence. Always prioritize node size over node color.

### Charts First

- Always start with charts before looking at Heatseeker.
- Charts create the thesis. Heatseeker confirms or challenges it.

| Charts answer | Heatseeker answers |
|---|---|
| Where is support and resistance? | Is dealer positioning supporting this level? |
| What is the trend? | Where is the real pressure? |
| Are we ranging or breaking out? | |

### Process

1. **Start with charts:** Identify structure. Is it a trend or a range?
2. **Check the map:** Where is the King Node? Where are the floors and ceilings?
3. **Identify the regime:** Does price look controlled (positive gamma) or fast (negative gamma)?
4. **Define the type of day:** Range, trend, or whipsaw? Context matters in judging the type of day.
5. **Set expectations:** Should levels hold? Should you expect range-bound action? Should you expect major reversals? If the maps support rejection off major levels, expect a major rejection.

## Key Takeaways

- Regime defines behavior, not direction
- Negative gamma = faster, expanding markets
- Types of days come from regime + structure
- Charts always come first
- Heatseeker confirms, it does not replace charts
