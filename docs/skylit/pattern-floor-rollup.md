# Pattern: Floor Roll-Up

## Definition

A floor rolling up is a positioning event where Market Makers shift the map upward from underneath price. It is strong presumptive evidence of a bullish thesis.

When a floor rolls up:
- Downside structure is being lifted
- Downside nodes decrease in value
- The floor moves to a higher strike

**Meaning:** The market has less room to travel lower than it had before. The map is tightening from underneath price, and the downside is being taken away. This is bullish structure improving under the surface.

**Analogy:** If the market had a trap door five floors below it, and Market Makers move that trap door up two floors, the downside just got smaller.

**Question to ask yourself:** If downside opportunity is shrinking, why am I still leaning bearish?

## What a Rolling Floor Changes

1. It reduces downside distance
2. It improves long-side asymmetry
3. It weakens the bear thesis

## What a Rolling Floor Is NOT

- Price breaking above resistance
- Price reclaiming a level
- A continuation trigger
- A breakout

## How to Read a Rolling Floor

1. **Establish the current downside map.** Start with the existing floor and downside targets. Ask:
   - Where is the nearest true floor?
   - How much room exists below spot?
   - Is there meaningful downside asymmetry?
2. **Watch rate of change.** Look for rapid accumulation, rapid unwinding, and rapid reshuffles. If the lower floor starts fading and a new one appears closer to price at a higher strike, that is the tell. The map is being pulled upward from underneath.
3. **Check whether downside targets are compressing.** This is the real confirmation. If lower targets are disappearing or becoming less valuable, the bearish path is getting weaker.
4. **Reassess bias.** Do not ask, "Can price still go lower?" Ask, "Is the map still paying me to be bearish?" If the answer is no, your bias needs to change.
5. **Wait for the A+ setup.** A rolling floor is not the trade by itself. It informs the bias. The trade still needs:
   - Chart structure
   - Heatseeker confluence
   - Asymmetric risk-to-reward
   - A clear entry point off a deflection of a key node

## General Rules (Apply to Both Floor and Ceiling Rolls)

### Rolling Is a Positioning Event

- Rolling happens when Market Makers begin shifting the map itself.
- Watch changing dealer exposure, reshuffles, and node rate of change. These changes reshape bias intraday and across sessions.
- Neither a floor roll nor a ceiling roll should be treated as a continuation play.
- Use Heatseeker to read changing dealer intent and adapt to reshuffles as they happen, not as a prediction machine.

### Rolling vs. Other Concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rolling | A positioning shift |
| Gamma flip | Price breaks and holds above a key node, which may then become a floor. This is a separate framework. |
| Continuation chasing | Not condoned |

Skylit doctrine: trade reversals at floors and ceilings, avoid midpoint noise, and do not build a strategy around chasing continuation.

### Gamma Context

Gamma regime changes how clean rolling looks.

| Positive Gamma | Negative Gamma |
|---|---|
| Slower transitions | Faster reshuffles |
| Cleaner compression | More violent map changes |
| Easier-to-read floors and ceilings | Less forgiving execution |
| Better-defined extremes | Greater need for cross-index confirmation |

Rolling matters in both regimes. In negative gamma, it can happen faster and punish hesitation harder.

### Relationship to Node Deflections and Tap Degradation

- Fresh nodes matter more than nodes that have already been interacted with. Once a level has been used, its influence drops and the probability of reversion declines.
- Rolling adds a second question. The trader moves from "Did the node bounce?" to "Is the map still offering the same opportunity set?"
- This is Tier 3 thinking.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Staying bearish while downside targets shrink:** This is stale thinking.
2. **Confusing rolling with gamma flips:** If price breaks and reclaims, that is a separate framework.
3. **Treating the old map like it still matters:** Heatseeker updates dynamically. Your bias must update with it.
4. **Chasing continuation:** Trade reversals at extremes, not blind continuation.
5. **Ignoring confluence:** SPX, SPY, and QQQ need to agree more often than not. Mixed reads lower confidence.

## Key Takeaways

- Rolling floors are positioning shifts
- A rolling floor means downside is shrinking and the floor is moving higher
- Rolling floors are bullish because they remove downside opportunity
- This is not a continuation pattern
- This is not a gamma flip
- The trade is still the reversal at the edge, with asymmetry
